01 / 07
The problem

Space missions are
a balancing act.

A more powerful instrument can require more energy, increase launch mass and consume a larger budget. Students rarely get to test all these decisions together.

How can we make engineering trade-offs tangible?

02 / 07
Our solution

An orbital expedition
you can play.

Assemble your spacecraftTest the power systemSimulateLearn & iterate

Create your own satellite, test its engineering trade-offs, then pilot it through a 2.5D orbital expedition.

Runs in a browser, without an account or API key.

03 / 07
The player experience

Every choice changes
your mission.

DESIGN

Place modules on a 5×5 deck. Check connectivity, mass balance and power. Save, compare and share your designs.

SEE THE CONSEQUENCES

Vary imaging duty and eclipse duration on the power bench, then launch your actual assembled craft into the game.

04 / 07
Demonstration

Failure becomes feedback.

LIVE EXPEDITION

Six packets to collect, three hull points and 150 seconds to complete the mission.

Only delivered science counts.

ENGINEERING TRADE-OFFS

Fast imaging uses more power. Bigger arrays recharge faster. Extra hardware slows the craft.

Refit and try a better route.

Arcade geometry and time are scaled for learning. The companion Engineering Lab explores the detailed design constraints.

05 / 07
Science & NASA resources

Grounded in systems thinking.

  • Mission lifecycle and systems trade-offs: NASA’s Design and Develop Science Missions, via the challenge resource summary.
  • Objective-to-instrument decisions: NASA’s Build It Yourself telescope game, via the challenge resource summary.
  • Circular-orbit period, simplified eclipse geometry, energy balance and data-return constraints.

All hardware values are illustrative. No NASA hardware dataset is imported. Model, sources, limitations and AI assistance: SOURCES.md.
